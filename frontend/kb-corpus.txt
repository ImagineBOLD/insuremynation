## Marine Cargo And Hull Cover (/marine-insurance)

Summary: Goods in transit change hands, modes and jurisdictions. Marine insurance follows the cargo the whole way, so one incident at sea does not land on your balance sheet.

Why it matters — Why Marine Insurance Is Important:
- Once a consignment leaves your warehouse it passes through carriers, ports, customs and handlers you do not control. Marine insurance covers the goods across that entire journey - sea, air, road and rail - not only the ocean leg.
- Carrier liability is capped, and the cap usually sits far below the value of the cargo. Without a marine policy, a container lost overboard, a fire in a hold or water damage in a monsoon port is your loss and your buyer's claim.
- Most letters of credit and Incoterms require cover in the seller's or the buyer's name before the shipment moves. We structure the policy to match your contract and your trade terms - and we appoint the surveyor when something goes wrong.

What you get:
- Warehouse To Warehouse: Cover that starts and ends at the door, not at the port gate
- Open Cover: Declare shipments as they move instead of buying one policy at a time
- Trade-Term Aligned: Written to match your Incoterms and your letter of credit
- Surveyor On Call: We appoint the surveyor and register the claim the day you report it

Cargo, Hull And Liability: Marine cover is not one product. We assemble the parts your trade actually needs - the cargo itself, the vessel it travels on, and the liabilities that come with moving other people's goods.

Cover includes:
- Marine cargo cover for imports, exports and domestic movement
- Institute Cargo Clauses A, B or C, chosen to fit the commodity
- Warehouse-to-warehouse transit across sea, air, road and rail
- Open cover and annual declaration policies for regular shippers
- Hull and machinery cover for owned or chartered vessels
- Protection and indemnity for crew, collision and wreck removal
- Freight forwarder and carrier legal liability
- War, strikes, riot and civil commotion extensions
- General average and salvage contributions
- Claims run with the surveyor, from survey report to settlement

## Claims and mis-selling support

InsureNation runs a support desk for policyholders in dispute with an insurer, whether or not the policy was bought through the firm. Complaints most often surface at the worst moment — at the time of a claim, when an illness or a loss is already being dealt with.

The desk handles:
- Mis-sold policies — a plan that never matched what was described, established in writing
- Claims sitting past the regulatory turnaround time, which get escalated
- Rejected claims — the repudiation letter is read and a case built for review
- Lapsed policies — revived with the least penalty possible
- Documentation, follow-ups and insurer coordination handled on the customer’s behalf
- Declined at underwriting — finding an insurer who will offer cover

Industry experts handhold the customer through the process to a satisfactory resolution.

A policy is only as good as the claim it pays. If an insurer has delayed, rejected or mis-sold, our industry experts take the case up on your behalf. To raise a case, tell us what happened in a few lines and keep the policy number and any letters from the insurer handy - a counsellor will call to collect the documents and set out the next steps. Your details stay with our claims desk, and there are no marketing calls.
